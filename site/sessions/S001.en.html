Session 001 - Monolithic Architecture Style

Goal

Main Topic

1. Definition and deployment unit

A monolith is a decision about the deployment unit: all functionality is packaged, versioned, and deployed as a single artifact. It is not a statement about code size or internal quality. "Monolith" is not a synonym for "poorly designed code" — you can have a well-delimited modular monolith (S022) or microservices as coupled as any neglected monolith.

2. Internal communication and data model

Modules communicate through in-process function calls, with no network serialization or need to handle partial network failures between internal modules. Multi-module transactions can rely on a local ACID transaction if they share a database, avoiding the complexity of Saga/TCC (S051, S052).

3. Monolith vs. "Big Ball of Mud"

The real anti-pattern is the absence of explicit internal boundaries (bounded contexts, S019), not the monolithic style. Decay is caused by lack of enforcement mechanisms, not by the choice of deployment style.

Sub topics

4. Scaling model

Vertical (more resources per instance) or horizontal by full replica. It does not allow scaling an individual module according to its own load, unlike microservices (S005).

5. Internal boundaries and modularity

Requires active mechanisms: package/module-level visibility in the language, architectural linters in CI (category: ArchUnit, dependency-cruiser, import-linter), or code review with an explicit checklist.

6. Trade-offs against distributed styles

DimensionMonolithMicroservices
Initial operational complexityLowHigh
Transactional consistencySimple (local ACID)Eventual (Saga, TCC)
Granular scalingNoYes

Blast radius riskAffects the whole processIsolable per service

Real world example

Verifiable fact: per Chris Richardson (microservices.io), several widely known services (Amazon.com, eBay, Netflix) began with a monolithic architecture before evolving toward service-based architectures. That source does not detail specific timelines or figures.

Illustrative scenario (pedagogical, not documented): a 4-person team building the MVP of a scheduling platform for medical clinics with a modular monolith of 3 packages (scheduling, billing, notifications).

Exercises

Exercise 1 — Architectural style decision for an MVP

Context: Principal Engineer of a 3-person team, last-mile logistics MVP, 8 weeks, domain not yet understood.

  1. List ≥4 context-specific arguments for the monolith and ≥2 against.
  2. Make the decision and justify it.
  3. Define the initial internal package structure (≥3).

Exercise 2 — Enforcing internal boundaries

Context: 12 months later, team grew from 3 to 12 engineers, unplanned cross imports between routing and notifications.

  1. Propose an enforcement mechanism (tool category and rule(s)).
  2. Define the violation/blocking/exception policy.
  3. Identify ≥2 additional "big ball of mud" warning signs.

Exercise 3 — Architecture Decision Record

Context: formalize the Exercise 1 decision.

  1. Write ADR-001 (Status, Context, Decision, Consequences).
  2. Include an explicit future review trigger.

See docs/adrs/ADR-001.md for the persisted ADR.

Principal Engineer Lens

Technical Perspective

In-process communication removes network overhead but also the failure isolation the network provides; boundary discipline determines whether the monolith stays maintainable.

Business Perspective

Lower cost/time to first release; the unmanaged risk is silent technical debt from boundary erosion.

AI Perspective

A synchronous call to an AI service inside the request flow introduces non-deterministic latency and a shared failure point; mitigation: an asynchronous pattern with a deterministic fallback.

Leadership Perspective

The style choice should be documented as an ADR, not adopted by default; Conway's Law (S117) already operates inside the monolith via internal package organization.

Reflection

A monolith is not an inferior architecture by definition — it is a legitimate decision about the deployment unit. What the industry often attributes to the "monolithic style" is, more precisely, the failure to maintain explicit internal boundaries and their enforcement over time.
